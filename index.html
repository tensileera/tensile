THE FUNDAMENTAL CONSTRAINT

THE TYRANNY OF CUBIC SCALING

Compressive structures carry load by adding mass. Under the square-cube law, this imposes a scaling ceiling.

Double a structure’s linear dimensions and its mass grows 8×, while its load-bearing cross-section grows 4×.

The largest production helicopter, the Mi-26, lifts roughly 20 tonnes of payload. Large offshore wind turbines stand on foundations exceeding 1,000 tonnes. Both limits follow from compressive scaling.

STRUCTURAL PRINCIPLE

LOAD PATH IN TENSION

The architecture carries load through centrifugally-stiffened tethers instead of compressive spars. Tension members do not buckle, so they need no reinforcement that grows faster than size.

Structural material scales linearly: a system twice as large requires twice the material; ten times as large, ten times the material.
